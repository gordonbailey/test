1 · Specification

Unit of analysisOrganization (account). Not campaign — the export has no per-campaign rows.
Outcomeraised_365 = Txn - Last 365, trailing-twelve-month dollars raised. Modelled as log(raised_365).
EstimatorOLS with cohort fixed effects (37 dummies, one dropped), HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust covariance.
Samplen = 3,161 of 6,759 (46.8%), complete-case on four required fields plus two activity floors.
Fit
Reference dateAS_OF_DATE = 2026-07-28, passed explicitly rather than read from the clock so re-runs are deterministic.
Heteroskedasticity is not incidental. Breusch–Pagan p ≈ 1e-50 — residual variance rises with scale, as fundraising residuals do. HC3 rather than HC1 because several cohorts are small (n = 31 at the top band) and HC3's leverage correction matters there.

2 · Field canonicalization

Three concepts had multiple conflicting sources. Resolutions and the evidence for each:

ConceptEvidenceResolution
Sector First letter of NTEE Code agrees with the letter in NTEE Category on 100% of non-null rows. The label is inconsistent: R–V render "Public, Societal Benefit", W renders "Public & Societal Benefit". Rebuild from the code letter via the 10 NTEE major groups. Z (NTEE's own "Unknown, Unclassified") is deliberately unmapped so it fails the eligibility gate rather than forming a spurious peer group.

Annual Revenue excluded from all sizing. A filed zero is treated as "no usable filing", not "no revenue".

Channel columns and Txn - Online are lifetime. Outcome is trailing-365 only. avg_gift = lifetime dollars ÷ lifetime gifts, used as an explanatory trait, never folded into the outcome.

3 · Eligibility and selection bias

Gates: sector non-null · org_revenue > 0 · raised_365 non-null · avg_gift computable · raised_365 ≥ $1,000 · gifts_lifetime ≥ 25.

The selection is not random, and this is the largest single threat to external validity. Excluded accounts skew smaller, newer and less engaged: the two binding gates are missing NTEE code (1,589) and missing IRS revenue (901), both of which correlate with being a small self-serve account. Every finding describes the benchmarkable half of the book. Nothing here should be extrapolated to the other 3,598 accounts, and the $60.0M "coachable gap" is a floor on the book-wide figure, not an estimate of it.

Paid Fundraising Staff (50% coverage) and # Individual Donors (61% among eligible) are carried as optional enrichment rather than gates — requiring them would have cut n to a few hundred. Where used as model terms they are cohort-median imputed with an imputation indicator retained.

4 · Cohort construction

Cohort = sector × size band. Bands on org_revenue, cut at 100k / 250k / 1M / 5M / 25M / 100M / 1B. Minimum cell size 30; thin cells back off to the size band.

BandnSpan (dex)Spearman(revenue, raised)
Identity vs reference population are separate columns on purpose. A backed-off account is labelled All sectors | <band> but is scored against every account in that band — ref_dimension selects which. The first implementation conflated them and scored 61 accounts against only each other while claiming an all-sector comparison; a from-scratch recomputation of band ranks is now asserted in the test suite.
Residual within-band size signal remains. Spearman(org_revenue, raised_365) inside bands runs +0.05 to +0.22, so a percentile carries a little size information on top of performance. The worst offender is $250k–$1M at +0.222 despite spanning only 0.60 dex — narrowing bands further will not fix it, because that correlation is a real capacity effect rather than a banding artifact.

5 · Model specification and diagnostics

TermCoefpVIFRole

Max VIF 4.1. Residual skew 0.13, excess kurtosis 1.05 — near-symmetric in logs, which is the reason for the log link. Cohort fixed effects absorb all time-invariant cohort characteristics, so lever coefficients are within-peer-group.

Every lever coefficient fell when history was added, and the drop is the interesting part. Average gift 0.640 → 0.432, recurring donors 0.474 → 0.340, active campaigns 0.256 → 0.063. The earlier specification was attributing "this organization was already a large fundraiser" to the levers. Campaign cadence in particular was mostly a scale proxy: it survives at p = 0.005 but with roughly a quarter of its former effect, and should be presented as a weak lever.

The ~0.05 gap to the shipped OLS is the price of keeping interpretable coefficients, which the recommendation layer requires. Nothing tried approached 0.9, and it is unlikely anything would: a viral campaign or a disaster-response spike is not predictable from firmographics.

7 · Recommendation mechanics

For account i and lever k: target = quantile of cohort peers at min(75, current percentile + 25). Projected effect = raised · exp(β_k · Δ_k), with bounds from the coefficient's 95% CI. Levers where the account already leads are dropped rather than shown as negative advice.

GuardThresholdFailure it prevents
Bounded stepMAX_LEVER_STEP_PCTL = 25Unbounded, a p5 → p75 jump on a small base produced a +2,560% projection.
Activation floorcohort_ratio < 0.10268 barely-onboarded accounts were being handed donor-upgrade strategies.
Exceptional ceilingcohort_ratio ≥ 5.0374 accounts. The top raiser (903× its median, $26 average gift by design) was advised to raise that gift for +$413M.
642 accounts (20%) get a scorecard but no ranked advice — both tails, by design. The model carries cohort fixed effects but no lever × position interaction, so it applies one elasticity to a cohort's median member and to an outlier three orders of magnitude above it alike. Suppression is the honest response to that limitation; interactions would be the fix, and they are not shipped.

7b · Scope of measurement: partial platform use

The outcome is dollars raised through this platform, not the organization's total fundraising. Nothing in the export identifies wallet share, so an organization that runs only its peer-to-peer with GoFundMe Pro is indistinguishable from one that runs everything and raises less.

Decomposing the gap

Accounts running one campaign type raise 2.414 log points less in lifetime dollars than accounts running all three. Because log(total) = log(types) + log(dollars per type), that splits exactly:

ComponentLog pointsShareInterpretation
Campaign-type count1.09946%Measurement artifact. Fewer types mechanically means fewer dollars ever recorded.
Dollars per type1.31554%Genuine. Narrow accounts are also smaller on the type they do run.

Per-channel like-for-like comparisons within size band corroborate the split: on donation pages narrow accounts run 0.27–0.53× of broad accounts (p 6.6e-14), on peer-to-peer 0.19–0.72× (p 3.9e-06), on ticketed 0.16–1.53× (p 3.1e-10) — but on Campaign Studio the difference is not significant (p 0.063) and reverses in three bands. So the per-channel component is real but uneven, and no single-account correction is defensible.

What was done about it, and what was not. Flagged, not corrected — platform_footprint is emitted per account and every narrow-footprint verdict carries a scope warning ahead of any gap figure, asserted in the test suite. It is not a model term, because adding it would let the regression absorb a measurement problem as if it were a behavioural one, and channel_breadth is already a lever, which makes it doubly ambiguous: for a broad-footprint account "add a channel" is advice, and for a narrow-footprint one it may be asking the organization to move a programme it deliberately runs elsewhere. Closing this properly needs a wallet-share input — self-reported total fundraising, or contributions revenue off the 990 — at which point raised_365 ÷ total contributions becomes a real denominator and the benchmark can be run on platform share rather than platform dollars.

Correction: report columns are not campaign types

The first version treated all seven channel dollar columns as parallel channels. They are not. Donation Page is where direct-giving traffic lands and Campaign Studio is one way to build such a page, so the pair is a destination and a builder for a single campaign type.

8 · Done well

  • The lifetime/trailing-window distinction was caught before modelling. The channel-sum reconciliation ($11.381B vs $11.373B on 95% of rows) is what established it. Had it been missed, the outcome would have silently mixed windows and every finding would be wrong in a way no diagnostic would have flagged.
  • Prior-period history was validated as non-overlapping before use — raised_365 ≤ raised_lifetime on 100% of rows, zero violations — which is what makes it a legitimate lagged predictor rather than leakage. This is asserted in the test suite, so it re-checks on every new extract.
  • Collinearity was caught by diagnostics that cross-validation could not see. The VIF-728 specification had the better CV R². Shipping it would have produced an unstable model with uninterpretable coefficients.
  • Both tails are suppressed rather than fudged. The failure modes are documented with the actual absurd outputs they produced, and tests assert advice never escapes the middle band.
  • The eligibility funnel is a first-class output. The cost of the completeness rule is visible in every report rather than hidden in a 47% sample.
  • 64 assertions cover the invariants, several written specifically to catch defects already found once: reference-population ranking, band span, ordinal formatting, driver polarity, excluded predictors.

9 · Done poorly, or not at all

  • Nothing here is causal, and the framing invites causal reading. The output is a ranked list of actions with dollar figures attached — that is an intervention framing built on observational associations. A CRM coefficient of ×1.32 is as much a capability marker as an effect. The correct next step is a holdout experiment on one lever (recurring giving), and until that runs every number in the recommendation layer is suggestive only.
  • Four defects reached a published artifact before being caught. Thin-cell accounts scored against the wrong reference population; open-ended size bands putting a $6.7B hospital with $26M foundations; cohort stats read from the label group rather than the reference population; and seven report columns treated as seven campaign types when two of them are a destination and a builder for one. Every one was found by inspection or by a user question, not by the tests that existed at the time. The tests were then written. That ordering is the wrong way round, and the last one is the clearest case: no amount of statistical care substitutes for knowing what a column means.
  • Cause area contributes almost nothing and is kept anyway. It explains 0.8% of variance in log raised against 16.7% for size band. It is retained because it makes the comparison credible to a nonprofit, which is a product judgment, not a statistical one. A defensible alternative — cohorting on operating profile instead — was never built.
  • Per-account prediction error is ×1.91 and the UI shows dollar figures to the dollar. Mitigated with caveats in every surface, but a confidence interval on each projection would be better than a sentence asking people not to over-read a precise-looking number.
  • No temporal validation. The export is a single snapshot, so the model is cross-sectional. There is no test of whether a lever moving in year t is followed by more raised in t+1, which is the question the recommendations actually imply. Two snapshots would enable it.
  • Imputation is cohort-median with an indicator, and unmodelled. Fine for employees (25% missing) but crude. Missingness is almost certainly not random — sparse fields correlate with self-serve accounts — and no sensitivity analysis was run on the imputation choice.
  • Ordinal buckets are mapped to midpoints. Paid Fundraising Staff and # Individual Donors arrive as ranges and become single numbers, with the open-ended top bucket ($>25,000$ donors → 30,000) essentially guessed. An ordered-categorical treatment would be more honest.
  • The exceptional threshold (5×) is a judgment call, not a derived cut. It behaves sensibly and its rationale is documented, but no sensitivity analysis over 3× / 5× / 10× was run to show how much the 374-account population moves.
  • Wording is duplicated between Python and JavaScript. The verdict templates exist in both so the CLI and the page can render them; the structural inputs are computed once in Python, but the sentences are mirrored by hand and can drift.
  • Partial platform use is flagged but not corrected, and it is the largest measurement threat. See §7b. 36% of accounts have a narrow recorded footprint and about half their apparent shortfall is channels we never see. A benchmark on platform share rather than platform dollars would fix it and needs one field the export does not have.
  • No campaign-level analysis, which is what was originally asked for. The data has no campaign records and no weekly series, so per-campaign realistic potential, momentum against cohort week-over-week, and donation growth/decay curves are all out of reach. This benchmarks organizations and channel mix.
