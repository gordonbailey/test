The problem

Positioning built on a guess about what competitors do collapses the first time a prospect knows more than the seller. So every claim here is graded: what a vendor says, what can be verified, and the difference marked on the page.

The seven promises the market sells. Raise more at the moment of giving · turn one-time into recurring · keep the donors you have · grow each donor's value · do more with the same team · know what to do next · know where you stand. Two are cross-cutting AI claims about protecting revenue and trust: Fundraise Up claims 98.8% of fraud blocked by AI, Blackbaud's Identity Thread claims 43% more duplicates found.

What came out of it

1 · An AI assistant in the admin console is now table stakes

Five assistant or agent launches between October 2025 and April 2026, joining long-standing assistants from Donorbox, DonorPerfect and Funraise. By giving season an admin evaluating platforms will expect a conversational assistant as standard.

"We have AI" no longer differentiates. Keep the February number: 92% adopt, 7% see major impact. The category is saturated with adoption and starved of demonstrated value.

2 · Autonomous, donor-touching outreach reached GA

Vendors have picked sides on whether AI talks to donors or only to staff.

Acts on donors
  • Blackbaud Development Agent, GA 17 March 2026. A named agent with personality and tone, cultivating assigned mid-tier portfolios by email and SMS under human oversight, every touch logged to the CRM. Claims up to 10× team capacity.
  • Bonterra Que: "it doesn't just advise, it acts." Acts in-platform today; outreach skills on the 2026 roadmap.
  • Salesforce Donor Support agent, beta, GA staged through 2026.
  • Fundraise Up, autonomous at checkout only. Nothing that reaches out.
Informs staff — the counter-position
  • Bloomerang markets the opposite posture: Penny suggests, the team decides. No record changes without confirmation, plus an org-level opt-out.
  • Virtuous Momentum drafts donor-facing outreach; a human sends it.
  • Donorbox, DonorPerfect, Funraise, Givebutter stay advisory and generative.

3 · Peer benchmarking exists, in four shapes

Consultant cohorts

Blackbaud donorCentrics, running since 1989, 400+ orgs. Five-year analyses, performance assessments, facilitated peer meetings. Deep and trusted; paid, opt-in, annual.

Annual published studies

Virtuous (771 orgs, Apr 2026), Bloomerang Giving Signals, M+R (~180 orgs). Free to read, invisible inside the daily workflow.

Quarterly insights new motion

Fundraise Up quarterly Pulse Check: anonymised transactions, 1,000+ orgs, four countries.

Claimed in-product claim

Bonterra Que, recommendations "backed by peer benchmarks" from 90,000+ campaigns, a network of 216,000 nonprofits and 46 million supporters. Depth unverifiable.

The gap is real-time and per-org. No vendor evidences transaction-level comparison inside the workflow, or an answer for organisations sitting below their peers. That describes what the fundraising benchmarking engine already does: cohorts of genuinely comparable organisations, a percentile per account, and a ranked list of levers for the ones behind.

5 · Trust language has converged; verifiability is unclaimed

Everyone says it

  • Bloomerang: "glass box, not black box", published principles, org opt-out, no training on customer data.
  • Blackbaud: Responsible AI framework, no customer data trains models.
  • Bonterra: human-led, see and interrogate and adjust every recommendation.
  • Virtuous: responsible-AI thought leadership.
  • Donorbox: SOC 2 Type II and PCI DSS Level 1 framing.

What none of them offers

  • Recommendations that arrive with their evidence.
  • Lift an organisation can check in its own numbers.
  • An honest record of misses.

All nine explain themselves. None lets a customer verify the result.

This matches the customer evidence. The interview synthesis found customers demanding proof of lift and refusing to trust asks they cannot inspect: Vine Maple turned Intelligent Ask on and could not tell whether it helped. Two independent directions, one gap.

What it does not cover

Public materials only, with no customer validation

Everything here is what vendors say about themselves, dated and sourced. Blackbaud's 10× capacity claim and Bonterra's 90,000-campaign benchmark depth are the kind of numbers that shrink on contact with a real deployment.

"Not found" does not mean "does not exist"

A capability absent from public marketing may be shipped and unmarketed, in private beta, or on a roadmap. Each cell records what the evidence showed in mid-July 2026.

Short shelf life

Eight launches in twelve months makes this a snapshot with a decay rate. It needs a re-run date and does not have one.

No win/loss or pricing evidence

Packaging is read off public pricing and announcements, so nothing here says which deals were lost to whom, or what customers paid.

Nine vendors is a choice, not the market

These are the platforms that come up in deals. General-purpose CRM, marketing automation and AI-native entrants too small to market are out of scope.

It stops at observation

This establishes what the market does, not what GoFundMe should do about it. The five questions below are genuinely open.

Pick this up next

Five questions the evidence raises and cannot answer.

1
Does the 50K NPO claim still position?
Bonterra markets peer benchmarks drawn from 216,000 nonprofits, so scale is not the differentiator. What replaces it?
2
What intelligence is uniquely ours?
Predictive scoring is being commoditised through Dataro and DonorSearch. What can only we build, from data only we have?
3
Everyone says "trust us" — what is our answer?
Trust language has fully converged and verifiability is unclaimed by all nine. Either an opening or a trap.
4
Staff-facing or donor-facing?
The market has split. Which do our customers care about more, and how does that change as marketplace integration strengthens?
5
How do we help customers do more with the same team?
The loudest claim of the cycle. The market's answer is agents aimed at the tiers humans never covered.
