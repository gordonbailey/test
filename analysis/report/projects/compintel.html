The problem

Every vendor in the category now says "we have AI." That makes the marketing useless as a signal and leaves an obvious question unanswered: what has actually shipped, what is merely announced, and where is the genuine gap? Positioning built on a guess about what competitors do is positioning that collapses the first time a prospect knows more than the seller.

The discipline that makes this useful is the one most comp intel skips: separating what a vendor claims from what can be verified, and marking the difference on the page.

The seven promises the market is selling. Raise more at the moment of giving · turn one-time into recurring · keep the donors you have · grow each donor's value · do more with the same team · know what to do next · know where you stand. Two are cross-cutting AI claims about protecting revenue and trust — Fundraise Up claims 98.8% of fraud blocked by AI, Blackbaud's Identity Thread claims 43% more duplicates found.

What came out of it

Finding 1 — an AI assistant in the admin console became table stakes

Five assistant or agent launches between October 2025 and April 2026, joining long-standing assistants from Donorbox, DonorPerfect and Funraise. By giving season, an admin evaluating platforms will treat a conversational assistant as expected furniture.

So what. "We have AI" no longer differentiates. And the February adoption report is the number to keep: 92% adopt, 7% see major impact — the category is saturated with adoption and starved of demonstrated value.

Finding 2 — autonomous, donor-touching outreach crossed to GA

Vendors are now picking sides on whether AI talks to donors or only to staff. That divide is a live market fact, not a philosophical debate.

Acts on donors
  • Blackbaud Development Agent — GA 17 March 2026. A named agent with personality and tone; autonomous email and SMS cultivation of assigned mid-tier portfolios under human oversight, every touch logged to the CRM. Claimed up-to-10x team capacity.
  • Bonterra Que — "it doesn't just advise, it acts." Acts in-platform today; outreach skills on the 2026 roadmap.
  • Salesforce Donor Support agent — beta, GA staged through 2026.
  • Fundraise Up — autonomous on donors at checkout only (DCF, recurring upgrades); nothing that actively reaches out.
Informs staff — the counter-position
  • Bloomerang markets the opposite posture explicitly: Penny suggests, the team decides. No record changes without confirmation, and an org-level opt-out.
  • Virtuous Momentum drafts donor-facing outreach; a human sends it.
  • Donorbox, DonorPerfect, Funraise, Givebutter remain advisory and generative only.

Where the agents are actually aimed

Not at every donor tier. The market has made an implicit bet about which relationships AI is allowed to own.

So what. The market's implicit bet: AI develops the relationships humans never got to — mid-tier, small and lapsed — while major gifts stay human. That is where "do more with the same team" actually points.

Finding 3 — peer benchmarking exists, in four shapes

Consultant cohorts

Blackbaud donorCentrics — running since 1989, 400+ orgs. Five-year analyses, performance assessments, facilitated peer benchmark meetings. Deep and trusted; paid, opt-in, annual.

Annual published studies

Virtuous (771 orgs, Apr 2026), Bloomerang Giving Signals, M+R (~180 orgs). Free to read; invisible inside the daily workflow.

Quarterly insights new motion

Fundraise Up quarterly Pulse Check — anonymized transactions, 1,000+ orgs, four countries.

Claimed in-product claim

Bonterra Que — recommendations "backed by peer benchmarks" from 90,000+ campaigns; a network of 216,000 nonprofits and 46 million supporters. Depth unverifiable.

So what — and this one is already actionable. No vendor evidences real-time, per-org, transaction-level comparison inside the workflow, nor an answer for organizations sitting below their peers. That is a precise description of what the fundraising benchmarking engine does: cohorts of genuinely comparable organizations, a percentile per account, and a ranked list of levers for the ones that are behind. The gap this finding identifies has a working prototype attached to it.

Finding 5 — trust language has converged; verifiability remains unclaimed

Everyone says it

  • Bloomerang — "glass box, not black box"; published principles, org opt-out, no training on customer data.
  • Blackbaud — Responsible AI framework; no customer data trains models.
  • Bonterra — human-led; see, interrogate and adjust every recommendation.
  • Virtuous — responsible-AI thought leadership.
  • Donorbox — SOC 2 Type II and PCI DSS Level 1 framing.

What no one offers

  • Recommendations that arrive with their evidence.
  • Lift an organization can check in its own numbers.
  • An honest record of misses.

Explanation is everywhere in market positioning. Verification is nowhere. (Observation, not recommendation — the deck is deliberate about that.)

This lines up exactly with the customer evidence. The interview synthesis found customers demanding proof of lift and refusing to trust asks they cannot inspect — Vine Maple turned Intelligent Ask on and could not tell whether it helped. The market gap and the customer ask are the same gap, arrived at from two independent directions.

What it does not cover

Public materials only — no customer validation of any claim

Everything here is what vendors say about themselves, dated and sourced. Not one claim has been checked against a customer who uses the feature. Blackbaud's up-to-10x capacity claim and Bonterra's 90,000-campaign benchmark depth are exactly the kind of numbers that shrink under contact with a real deployment.

"Not found" is not the same as "does not exist"

The snapshot is explicit about this, and it matters. A capability absent from public marketing may be shipped and unmarketed, in private beta, or on a roadmap. The cell records what the evidence showed in mid-July 2026 and nothing more.

It has a short shelf life, by its own argument

The central finding is that the pace is the strategic fact — eight tracked launches in twelve months. A landscape moving that fast makes this a snapshot with a decay rate, not a reference document. It needs a re-run date, and it does not have one.

No win/loss or pricing evidence

Packaging is read off public pricing and announcements. Nothing here says what deals were actually lost to which competitor, or what customers paid — which is the evidence that would turn a positioning argument into a business case.

Nine vendors is a choice, not the market

The nine cover the platforms that come up in deals. Adjacent categories — general-purpose CRM, marketing automation, the AI-native entrants too small to have marketing yet — are out of scope, and the third-party layer is only represented where it embeds into one of the nine.

It deliberately stops short of recommending

Finding 5 flags this in its own footnote: "observation, not recommendation." The five questions for the room are genuinely open, which is honest — but it does mean this deck hands the hardest decisions to somebody else.

Pick this up next

The five questions the evidence raises and cannot answer. These are the agenda, in the deck's own framing.

1
Does the 50K NPO claim still position?
If Bonterra already markets peer benchmarks drawn from 216,000 nonprofits, scale is not the differentiator we thought it was. What replaces it?
2
What intelligence is uniquely ours?
Predictive scoring is being commoditized through Dataro and DonorSearch — anyone can buy it. So what can only we build, from data only we have?
3
Everyone says "trust us" — what is our answer?
Trust language has fully converged. Verifiability is unclaimed by all nine. That is either an opening or a trap, and the deck deliberately does not say which.
4
Staff-facing or donor-facing?
The market has split. Which do our customers care about more, and how does that change as marketplace integration strengthens? What tools help customers take advantage of marketplace donors?
5
How do we help customers do more with the same team?
This is the loudest claim of the cycle across every vendor. The market's answer is agents aimed at the tiers humans never covered — mid-level, small, lapsed.
