The problem

A sales pitch built on a guess about what a competitor does falls apart the first time a customer knows more than the seller. And right now the marketing tells you nothing, because everyone claims the same things.

So every claim here is graded: what the vendor says, what can actually be checked, and the difference marked on the page.

What came out of it

1 · An AI assistant is now the price of entry

Five launched between October 2025 and April 2026, on top of three that already existed. By giving season, anyone comparing platforms will expect one as standard.

"We have AI" no longer sets anyone apart. Hold on to the February number: 92% of nonprofits have adopted AI and 7% say it made a real difference. Everyone has it. Almost nobody can show it working.

2 · AI that contacts donors directly is now live

Vendors have taken sides on whether AI talks to donors or only to staff.

Acts on donors
  • Blackbaud's Development Agent, live since March 2026. It has a name and a personality, emails and texts mid-level donors on a fundraiser's behalf, and logs everything. Claims to make a team 10× more productive.
  • Bonterra's Que: "it doesn't just advise, it acts." Today it acts inside the platform; donor outreach is on the roadmap.
  • Salesforce Donor Support agent, beta, GA staged through 2026.
  • Fundraise Up acts only at the checkout. It never contacts anyone.
Advises staff — the opposite bet
  • Bloomerang sells the opposite: Penny suggests, the team decides. Nothing changes without a human confirming it.
  • Virtuous Momentum drafts donor-facing outreach; a human sends it.
  • Donorbox, DonorPerfect, Funraise, Givebutter stay advisory and generative.

Which donors the AI is pointed at

Four donor tiers, and who is going after each
Donor tierWho owns itWhat the market is doing
Major & principal giftsHuman-ledStill handled by people. AI just preps and drafts. Nobody lets AI contact these donors on its own.
Mid-level portfoliosAgents — GAWhere the fight is. These donors are worth too little to justify a fundraiser's time and too much to ignore, so the AI takes them.
Small & recurringAutomationAutomation, not relationships.
Lapsed & never-assignedScoring → agentsPredicting who is about to stop giving, and who could be won back. Relationships nobody was going to build anyway.
The market's bet is that AI builds the relationships people never had time for, while the biggest donors stay human. That is what "do more with the same team" actually means.

3 · Peer benchmarking exists, in four shapes

Paid consultant groups

Blackbaud donorCentrics, going since 1989, 400+ organizations. Deep and trusted, but you pay for it, you opt in, and it happens once a year.

Annual reports

Virtuous (771 organizations), Bloomerang, M+R (~180). Free to read, and nowhere near you while you work.

Quarterly reports new

Fundraise Up publishes every quarter from anonymized donations across 1,000+ organizations.

Claimed inside the product unproven

Bonterra Que says its advice is "backed by peer benchmarks" from 90,000+ campaigns. No way to check how deep that goes.

The gap is live comparison, for your own organization. Nobody can show it happening inside the product, and nobody has an answer for an organization sitting below its peers. That is exactly what the benchmarking engine does.

5 · Everyone promises trust. Nobody offers proof.

Everyone says it

  • Bloomerang: "glass box, not black box", published principles, and no training on customer data.
  • Blackbaud: Responsible AI framework, no customer data trains models.
  • Bonterra: human-led, and you can question or change every recommendation.
  • Virtuous: articles about responsible AI.
  • Donorbox: leads with its security certifications.

What none of them offers

  • Recommendations that arrive with their evidence.
  • Lift an organization can check in its own numbers.
  • An honest record of misses.

All nine explain themselves. Not one lets a customer check the result.

The customers said the same thing. The interviews found people demanding proof and refusing to trust what they cannot inspect. Two separate pieces of research, one gap.

What it does not cover

This is what vendors say about themselves

No customer confirmed any of it. Blackbaud's 10× productivity claim and Bonterra's benchmark depth are exactly the kind of numbers that shrink when a real customer tries them.

"Not found" does not mean "does not exist"

Something missing from public marketing might be shipped and unmarketed, in private beta, or coming next month. Each entry records what could be seen in mid-July 2026.

It goes stale fast

Eight launches in twelve months. This is a snapshot, and it needs a date to be redone. It does not have one.

No pricing or deal evidence

Packaging comes off public pricing pages. Nothing here says which deals were lost to whom, or what anyone actually paid.

It stops at observation

This says what the market is doing, not what GoFundMe should do about it. The five questions below are genuinely open.

Pick this up next

Five questions the evidence raises and cannot answer.

1
Does "50,000 nonprofits" still mean anything?
Bonterra claims benchmarks from 216,000. Scale is no longer the argument. What replaces it?
2
What can only we build?
Anyone can buy prediction now. What could we build from data nobody else has?
3
Everyone says "trust us". What do we say?
All nine promise trust and none offers proof. That is either an opening or a trap.
4
Should our AI talk to staff or to donors?
The market has split down the middle. Which do our customers actually want?
5
How do we help a small team do more?
The loudest promise in the market. Everyone's answer is AI aimed at the donors nobody had time for.
