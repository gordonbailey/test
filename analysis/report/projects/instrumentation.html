The problem

The existing metrics were logins, orgs and revenue: a scoreboard with no game film.

There are two ways to design the replacement badly. Measuring clicks produces dashboards nobody acts on. Measuring only outcomes says the campaign-creation funnel is broken without saying which step to fix.

What was done

Layer 1

Health monitoring — lagging indicators

Org- and admin-level activation, engagement and retention. Tells you whether Pro is healthy overall and whether a launch moved the needle. Explicitly framed as the scoreboard, not the game film — it gives no early signal and is not meant to.

Layer 2

Core workflow instrumentation — leading indicators

Per-workflow questions and KPIs across eight areas. This is where early signal lives and where most product decisions actually get made. Every KPI is paired with the decision it informs — a metric with no decision attached does not earn a row.

First, define "active admin" — three tiers

Each tier subsumes the one below, so the ladder is cumulative rather than exclusive.

TierNameQualifying behavior
A1PresentLogged in / session started.
A2WorkingPerformed at least one core action — viewed a report or campaign insights, searched or viewed a transaction or supporter, opened the campaign builder or editor.
A3Value-creatingPerformed at least one value action — published a campaign, exported/shared/saved a report, processed a refund or offline donation, modified a recurring plan, invited an admin, connected an integration, edited org settings.

Active org = an org with at least one A2+ admin in the period. MANPO (monthly active nonprofits, per the GFM Channel ask) = A2+ at org level. A ramped customer requires both A3 status and ≥90 days as a customer — so anyone at A1–A2, or at A3 for under 90 days, is by definition not ramped.

"Active users" is the number most often quoted and least often defined. A1 counts someone who logged in and bounced; A3 counts someone who published a campaign. Reporting them as one figure is how a product looks healthy while nobody does anything in it.

Then work out who is doing the work

Surveying every admin is not practical, so role assignments plus behaviour approximate the personas.

These are the same nine personas from the interview research, reused so a qualitative finding becomes a measurable one. All nine were coded from the 39 interviews in the interview synthesis.

1.5How many orgs and admins are actively working in Pro?A2/A3 active orgs and admins (D/W/M); stickiness ratio (WAO/MAO).
1.6How broad is engagement inside an org?Median number of core workflow areas touched per org per month; A2+ admins per org. Breadth is the best proxy for "Pro is the operating system, not just a form builder."
1.7Is the org building habits?% of orgs A2-active in ≥3 of the last 4 weeks — "habitual orgs". The candidate north-star: it is what retention looks like before retention happens.
1.8What percent of campaigns are Yield Ready?% with campaign scoring, goal set, IAA and progress bar enabled. Definition agreed between Product, GGTM and CX.
Retention
1.9Do orgs come back?30/60/90-day org retention (≥1 A2 admin returning), cohorted by signup month and segment.
1.10How does stickiness evolve from Day 1 → 30 → 90?Admin-level retention curves by tenure cohort. Where the curve flattens is the "habit formed" point.
1.11Are we losing the silent churners?% of Self-Serve/Essentials orgs with zero A2 activity for 30+ days. The competitive early-warning metric — Grace-level departures happen silently and switching cost is roughly zero.
1.12Are orgs getting value?% of orgs with positive YoY growth that consistently meet campaign goals, p50/p90.
Speed, reliability, trust

Two research findings are wired into the measurement design. Reporting carries the instruction that low My Reports traffic means workflow avoidance rather than low need, matching what the interview synthesis found from the other direction. Exports are treated as an ambiguous signal: they can mean healthy downstream usage, or that the platform cannot answer the question natively. So the instruction is to track exports without treating them as success.
Integrations-drive-stickiness is a hypothesis, and 4.6 is designed to kill it. It is worth testing, and building a retention strategy on the untested correlation would be expensive.

What it does not cover

Nobody has confirmed the tiers are measurable

Whether A1/A2/A3 match what is distinguishable in mParticle events today is the first open question, and it is load-bearing. If A2 and A3 cannot be told apart in the event stream, the definitions are a proposal rather than a spec and most of Layer 1 cannot be computed.

The persona proxies are unvalidated

Whether they can be built from Cerberus role assignments plus behaviour, and at what confidence, is open. Adam (Agency) is "largely invisible today" because of shared logins, which also means agency-served orgs are misattributed across every admin-level metric.

Shared logins will pollute admin-level metrics

A known-agency flag may be needed. Until there is one, any admin-level figure for an agency-managed org measures a login rather than a person.

Cohort hygiene is unresolved for migrated orgs

Classy-migrated and net-new orgs have different day zeros, which breaks every time-to-X metric in the activation section if they are pooled. No decision yet.

The instrumented-versus-not audit is outstanding

Which metrics already exist in the Hex v1 dashboard and which need net-new events is open, owned by Stephen. Until it lands the proposal has no cost attached.

No retention denominator has been chosen

Org, admin, or both. It changes the headline number materially, and deciding late invites two teams to quote different retention figures.

No targets, and no prioritisation

This defines what to measure and why it matters. It does not say what good looks like for any metric, and it does not rank the eight workflow areas, so it is silent on whether processor setup or reporting gets instrumented first. That ordering is where the cost sits.
