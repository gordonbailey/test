The problem

Pro measured logins, organizations and revenue. That tells you the score at the end of the month. It does not tell you which part of the product is going wrong while there is still time to fix it.

There was also no agreed definition of an "active admin", so two people could pull activation numbers from the same database and both be right.

Measuring clicks would give dashboards nobody acts on. Measuring only outcomes tells you campaign creation is broken without telling you which step to fix. This needed both.

What was done

Layer 1

The scoreboard

Thirteen measures of whether Pro is healthy: how fast new organizations get going, how many come back, how many quietly stop showing up. Good for telling you whether a launch worked. Useless as an early warning, and not meant to be.

Layer 2

The early warning

Eight parts of the product, each measured in a way that shows a problem while it is still small. Every measure names the decision it would change. A measure that changes no decision does not get a row.

First, define "active admin"

Three levels. Each one includes everyone below it.

TierNameQualifying behavior
1PresentLogged in.
2WorkingDid something real: opened a report, looked up a donation or a supporter, opened the campaign builder.
3ProducingFinished something: published a campaign, exported a report, processed a refund, invited a colleague, connected another system.

An active organization has at least one admin at level 2 or above. A ramped customer needs level 3 and ninety days on the platform, so nobody counts as ramped in their first quarter.

Why this matters more than it sounds. Level 1 counts someone who logged in and left. Level 3 counts someone who published a campaign. Report those as one "active users" number and the product looks healthy while nobody does anything in it.

Then work out who is doing the work

You cannot survey every admin, so their job title and what they click stand in for it. These are the same nine people types found in the 39 interviews, so a research finding becomes something you can count.

What came out of it

The scoreboard: thirteen questions

Four of them do most of the work. The full list is below.

The gate

How long until a new organization can take a donation at all?

Nothing else can happen until the payment processor is switched on. Every other activation number sits behind this one.

The candidate headline

Is the organization building a habit?

The share active in at least three of the last four weeks. This is what retention looks like before retention happens.

The early warning

Are we losing people quietly?

Smaller customers with no activity for 30 days. They leave without complaining, and it costs them nothing to switch.

The depth check

How much of the product does one organization use?

The best signal that Pro is the system they run on rather than a form builder they visit.

The early warning: eight parts of the product

Each one names the sharpest question in it, and the decision that question would change.

WhereThe question worth asking
Payment setup
everyone, day one		The most important funnel in the product: no processor, no donations. Where do organizations get stuck, and how many are sitting in verification limbo?
Building a campaign
most admins		How many start from a template versus copying an old campaign? Copying wins today because templates break when a page has custom code, so the copy rate really measures whether templates work.
Reporting
data and director types		Is exporting replacing analysis in the product? Watch the ratio, and how many export the same report three times a month.
Day-to-day donation admin
the highest-value group		How long, and how many clicks, to do a refund or fix a monthly gift. This turns "reduce clicks" into something you can measure.
Team and permissionsAre agencies sharing one login? One account, many devices. An agency asked for this directly, and it is also a security problem.
Connections to other systemsDo connected organizations stay longer? Everyone believes they do. This is designed to test it rather than assume it.
The GoFundMe channelIs it bringing in money that would not have arrived anyway?
Recommendation cardsDo organizations that turn them on raise more? And how many people dismiss them?
Two research findings are built into the design. First, low traffic to the reports page means people are avoiding it, not that they do not need reports — which is what the interviews found from the other direction. Second, an export can mean healthy use or it can mean the product could not answer the question, so exports get counted but never treated as a win.

What it does not cover

Nobody has confirmed the three levels can actually be measured

Whether our analytics can tell level 2 from level 3 today is the first open question, and everything rests on it. If it cannot, these definitions are a proposal rather than a spec and most of the scoreboard cannot be calculated.

Shared agency logins will distort every per-person number

Agencies log in as one account, so the agency persona is nearly invisible and any per-admin figure for an agency-run organization counts a login rather than a person. It needs a flag.

Migrated customers have a different starting line

Organizations that came over from Classy and brand-new ones have different day zeros. Pool them and every "time to first X" number breaks. Not yet decided.

Nobody has counted what already exists

Which of these can be built from data we already collect, and which need new tracking, is still open and owned by Stephen. Until that lands, the proposal has no price on it.

No targets, and no order

This says what to measure and why. It does not say what good looks like for any of it, and it does not say which of the eight to build first. That ordering is where the cost sits.

One change fixes three problems: agencies become visible, the shared-login question becomes answerable, and agency logins stop quietly skewing every per-person number.
5

Payment setup is the obvious one: no processor, no donations, and it is a simple funnel — the cheapest place to prove this works.
