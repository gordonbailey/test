The problem

You cannot tell whether a launch worked if the only numbers available are the scoreboard. Pro had health metrics — logins, orgs, revenue — and almost nothing that would give early signal on a specific workflow. Worse, "active admin" had no agreed definition, so two people could report different activation numbers from the same database and both be right.

Two failure modes to design against. Measuring clicks, which produces dashboards nobody acts on. And measuring only outcomes, which tells you the campaign-creation funnel is broken without telling you which step to fix.

What was done

Layer 1

Health monitoring — lagging indicators

Org- and admin-level activation, engagement and retention. Tells you whether Pro is healthy overall and whether a launch moved the needle. Explicitly framed as the scoreboard, not the game film — it gives no early signal and is not meant to.

Layer 2

Core workflow instrumentation — leading indicators

Per-workflow questions and KPIs across eight areas. This is where early signal lives and where most product decisions actually get made. Every KPI is paired with the decision it informs — a metric with no decision attached does not earn a row.

First, define "active admin" — three tiers

Each tier subsumes the one below, so the ladder is cumulative rather than exclusive.

TierNameQualifying behavior
A1PresentLogged in / session started.
A2WorkingPerformed at least one core action — viewed a report or campaign insights, searched or viewed a transaction or supporter, opened the campaign builder or editor.
A3Value-creatingPerformed at least one value action — published a campaign, exported/shared/saved a report, processed a refund or offline donation, modified a recurring plan, invited an admin, connected an integration, edited org settings.

Active org = an org with at least one A2+ admin in the period. MANPO (monthly active nonprofits, per the GFM Channel ask) = A2+ at org level. A ramped customer requires both A3 status and ≥90 days as a customer — so anyone at A1–A2, or at A3 for under 90 days, is by definition not ramped.

Why the tiers matter more than they look. "Active users" is the number most often quoted and least often defined. A1 counts someone who logged in and bounced; A3 counts someone who published a campaign. Reporting them as one figure is how a product looks healthy while nobody does anything in it.

Then work out who is doing the work

You cannot survey every admin, so role assignments plus behavior approximate the personas.

These are the same nine personas from the interview research. The framework reuses them deliberately, which is what lets a qualitative finding become a measurable one — see the interview synthesis, where all nine were coded from 39 interviews.

Layer 1 — the health scoreboard

Activation
1.1How long after signup until an org can actually accept a donation?Median time from org creation to payment processor enabled — "time to able-to-fundraise". The activation gate for everything else.
1.2How long until the org gets its first real value?Median time to first campaign published; median time to first donation received.
1.3What share of new orgs completes the core setup bundle by Day 7 / 30?% of new orgs with processor enabled + first campaign published + ≥1 report viewed.
1.4How many orgs invite a second admin?% inviting a second admin within X days of first login, by admin hierarchical type.
Engagement
1.5How many orgs and admins are actively working in Pro?A2/A3 active orgs and admins (D/W/M); stickiness ratio (WAO/MAO).
1.6How broad is engagement inside an org?Median number of core workflow areas touched per org per month; A2+ admins per org. Breadth is the best proxy for "Pro is the operating system, not just a form builder."
1.7Is the org building habits?% of orgs A2-active in ≥3 of the last 4 weeks — "habitual orgs". The candidate north-star: it is what retention looks like before retention happens.
1.8What percent of campaigns are Yield Ready?% with campaign scoring, goal set, IAA and progress bar enabled. Definition agreed between Product, GGTM and CX.
Retention
1.9Do orgs come back?30/60/90-day org retention (≥1 A2 admin returning), cohorted by signup month and segment.
1.10How does stickiness evolve from Day 1 → 30 → 90?Admin-level retention curves by tenure cohort. Where the curve flattens is the "habit formed" point.
1.11Are we losing the silent churners?% of Self-Serve/Essentials orgs with zero A2 activity for 30+ days. The competitive early-warning metric — Grace-level departures happen silently and switching cost is roughly zero.
1.12Are orgs getting value?% of orgs with positive YoY growth that consistently meet campaign goals, p50/p90.
Speed, reliability, trust
1.13Are the surfaces admins live in fast and correct?p50/p90 load time for reports and campaign insights; error rate in core workflows; activity-feed lag versus transaction history — the feed posts on payout rather than gift initiation, so admins bypass it.

Two research findings are wired directly into the measurement design. Reporting carries the explicit instruction that low My Reports traffic means workflow avoidance, not low need — the same interpretation the interview synthesis reached from the other direction. And exports are treated as an ambiguous signal: they can mean healthy downstream usage or "the platform can't answer my question natively." The instruction is to track it, don't valorize it, which is the difference between a metric and a vanity metric.
One hypothesis is named as a hypothesis. The integrations-drive-stickiness idea is flagged in the doc as Gordon's own, "testable and worth testing properly", and 4.6 is designed to be able to kill it. Building a retention strategy on an untested correlation is the expensive version of this mistake.

What it does not cover

Nobody has confirmed the tiers are measurable

The first open question is whether A1/A2/A3 match what is actually distinguishable in mParticle events today, or whether new events are needed to separate A2 from A3. This is load-bearing: if A2 and A3 cannot be told apart in the event stream, the definitions section is a proposal rather than a spec, and most of Layer 1 cannot be computed.

The persona proxies are unvalidated

Whether they can be built from Cerberus role assignments plus behavior — and at what confidence — is an open question. The framework is honest that Adam (Agency) is "largely invisible today" because of shared logins, which also means agency-served orgs are systematically misattributed across every admin-level metric.

Shared logins will pollute admin-level metrics either way

Flagged explicitly: a known-agency flag may be needed. Until then, any admin-level figure for an agency-managed org is measuring a login, not a person.

Cohort hygiene is unresolved for migrated orgs

Orgs migrated from Classy and net-new orgs have different "day 0", which breaks every time-to-X metric in the activation section if they are pooled. No decision yet on how to handle it.

The instrumented-versus-not audit has not been done

Which of these already exist in the Hex v1 dashboard and which need net-new events is an open item owned by Stephen. So the doc cannot yet say what any of it costs to build — which is normally the first question asked of a metrics proposal.

No retention denominator has been chosen

Org, admin, or both dashboards — still open. It changes the headline number materially, and picking it late invites two teams to quote different retention figures.

No targets, and no prioritization

This defines what to measure and why it matters. It does not say what good looks like for any metric, and does not rank the eight workflow areas — so the reader cannot tell whether processor setup or reporting gets instrumented first. That ordering is where the real cost sits.
