The problem

Growth is getting narrower. Donation sizes are rising while donor counts are flat to down, which means the platform is monetising moments of generosity more efficiently rather than producing more generous people. The strategic question is whether there is a cohort worth building a programme around — and if so, which one.

There is. But the widely-quoted version of the finding is missing the number that makes it an argument.

"~40% of GDV" is not, by itself, evidence of anything. The first question any reader asks is "out of what share of donors?" If super donors were 40% of donors producing 40% of GDV, there would be no story at all. The answer is that they are 20% of donors producing 39% of GDV — a 1.9× over-index. Lead with 20/39, not 40.

What was done

Every figure below is queried from Amplitude (GFM v2 Prod, project 530253) over a trailing 12-month window, using the same super donor definition as the existing cohort: 2 or more eCommerce - Purchase events in a rolling 365 days. GDV is the sum of donation_amount.

DefinitionReused the existing cohort's rule rather than inventing one, so the numbers are comparable to what is already circulating
BasisGDV and gift counts are 12-month sums — both additive, so they divide cleanly
Donor countsDerived as gifts ÷ N per band, not read from a uniques query — see the basis trap below
Cross-checkDerived super donor count came to 7.38M against the cohort's own 7.12M — 3.7% apart
The basis trap, because it would have inverted the conclusion. Querying unique donors per band returns a monthly average of active donors, not an annual population. On that basis super donors look like ~37% of donors — because a 2+ donor is mechanically more likely to be active in any given month than a one-time donor. Divide 39% of GDV by 37% of donors and the concentration argument evaporates. The annual basis is both the honest one and the stronger one.

Average gift size falls as frequency rises — $94.63 at one gift, down to $77.27 at five. Super donors give more in total and less per gift. Two consequences worth planning for:

1. A successful frequency programme will reduce average donation size. Anyone tracking avg gift as a KPI will read success as failure. Say so before you start, not afterwards.
2. "Donation sizes are up ~28% while donor counts are flat" may be partly composition — the mix shifting toward one-time donors, who give the largest single gifts — rather than rising generosity.

The one-more-gift economics

Moving a donor from one gift to two adds $84.37 of annual GDV — the gap between $179.00 and $94.63. The addressable population is 29.2 million people.

$24.6M

1% of one-time donors give a second gift — 291,735 people.

$123M

5% convert — 1.46M people. Comparable to the deck's $87M estimate, on a firmer base.

$246M

10% convert — 2.92M people.

Why this beats "a 5% lift on super donor GDV = $87M." A 5% lift on an existing cohort is an assumption about a number nobody controls. A 1→2 conversion rate is a measurable behaviour with a 29-million-person base, a known baseline, and a known intervention window — see acquiring better donors for the 12.3% baseline and the three-week window.

What it does not cover

Three different GDV totals are in circulation, and this page does not resolve them

The strategy deck says $1.74B. The SuperDonor GDV by donation count spreadsheet sums to ~$1.55B. This page's Amplitude query gives $1.775B. The spreadsheet runs ~13% below the Amplitude figures in every band, which points to one systematic difference — most likely calendar 2025 versus a trailing 12-month window, or donation_amount versus a net/settled revenue field. The shares are stable across all three, so the 20/39 argument holds regardless. The absolute dollars should be reconciled to one source before any of them appears in a decision document.

GDV may not be currency-normalised

The purchase event carries donation_currency and donor_selected_currency_donation_amount alongside donation_amount. Whether the summed field is already converted to a single currency has not been verified. If it is not, the totals mix currencies. Again, shares survive this; absolute dollars may not.

The 6+ band's donor count is estimated

Bands 1–5 divide gifts by an exact N. The 6+ band has no single N, so its donor count (~403,000) is derived from the frequency distribution's gift-weighted average of ~9.7 gifts per donor. Everything downstream of that band — its per-donor figure of ~$751 — inherits the estimate.

This is consumer GoFundMe only

Project 530253 is GFM v2 Prod. Nothing here includes GoFundMe Pro (project 716579), so the analysis says nothing about nonprofit-side donor behaviour or how the two books interact.

Frequency is not the only definition of a valuable donor

The cohort rule is 2+ gifts in 365 days, which treats a donor giving $20 twice as a super donor and one giving $5,000 once as not. That is a defensible choice for a habit-formation strategy and a poor one for a major-gift strategy. It is a definition, not a discovery.

No causal claim is available here

Donors in higher-frequency bands are worth more per year. Nothing in this analysis shows that moving a donor between bands produces the value gap — the bands may simply contain different kinds of people. The $24.6M-per-percentage-point figures are the value of the observed gap, not a measured treatment effect. Only an experiment settles it.

Pre-agree that average gift size will fall
It falls monotonically with frequency. Get that in front of whoever owns the avg-donation KPI before the programme starts, or a success looks like a regression.
4
Design the experiment before the programme
A holdout on the 1→2 intervention is the only thing that converts the observed $84/donor gap into a defensible causal number. Without it this stays a correlation with a big multiplier attached.
