The problem

Growth is getting narrower. Donation sizes are rising while donor counts are flat to down, so the platform is monetizing moments of generosity more efficiently rather than producing more generous people. The question is whether there is a cohort worth building a program around, and which one.

Lead with 20/39, not 40. Repeat donors are 20% of donors producing 39% of GDV, a 1.9× over-index. The share of GDV on its own says nothing; paired with the share of donors it is the whole argument.

What was done

Every figure is queried from Amplitude (GFM v2 Prod, project 530253) over a trailing 12 months, using the same rule as the existing cohort: 2 or more eCommerce - Purchase events in a rolling 365 days. GDV is the sum of donation_amount. Donor counts are annual populations, derived as gifts ÷ N per band. The derived super donor count came to 7.38M against the cohort's own 7.12M, 3.7% apart.

Average gift size falls as frequency rises, from $94.63 at one gift to $77.27 at five. Repeat donors give more per year and less per gift. Two consequences to plan for:

1. A successful frequency program will reduce average donation size. Anyone tracking average gift as a KPI will read the win as a loss. Agree that in advance.
2. "Donation sizes are up ~28% while donor counts are flat" may be partly composition. One-time donors give the largest single gifts, so a mix shift toward them raises the average without anyone becoming more generous.

What one more gift is worth

Moving a donor from one gift to two adds $84.37 of annual GDV, the gap between $179.00 and $94.63. The addressable population is 29.2 million people.

Target the conversion rate, not the cohort. A 1→2 conversion rate is a behavior you can measure and move: a 29-million-person base, a 12.3% baseline, and a known three-week window. See acquiring better donors.

What it does not cover

Three GDV totals are in circulation

The strategy deck says $1.74B. The SuperDonor GDV by donation count spreadsheet sums to ~$1.55B. This page's Amplitude query gives $1.775B. The spreadsheet runs ~13% below Amplitude in every band, which points to one systematic difference, most likely calendar 2025 versus a trailing 12 months, or donation_amount versus a net settled field. The shares hold across all three, so the 20/39 split is safe. Reconcile the absolute dollars to one source before any of them lands in a decision document.

Donor counts are annual, and that choice matters

A uniques query in Amplitude returns a monthly average of active donors, not an annual population. On that basis repeat donors look like 37% of donors instead of 20%, because someone who gives twice a year is mechanically more likely to be active in any given month. The annual basis is the correct one for a 12-month GDV comparison.

GDV may not be currency-normalized

The purchase event carries donation_currency and donor_selected_currency_donation_amount alongside donation_amount. Whether the summed field is already converted to one currency is unverified. If it is not, the totals mix currencies. Shares survive this; absolute dollars may not.

The 6+ band's donor count is estimated

Bands 1–5 divide gifts by an exact N. The 6+ band has no single N, so its ~403,000 donors come from the distribution's gift-weighted average of ~9.7 gifts per donor. Its per-donor figure of ~$751 inherits that estimate.

Consumer GoFundMe only

Project 530253 is GFM v2 Prod. Nothing here includes GoFundMe Pro (project 716579), so it says nothing about nonprofit-side donor behavior or how the two books interact.

Frequency is one definition of a valuable donor

The rule of 2+ gifts in 365 days makes a donor who gives $20 twice a super donor and one who gives $5,000 once not. That works for a habit-formation strategy and fails for a major-gift strategy.

Nothing here is causal

Donors in higher-frequency bands are worth more per year. Nothing shows that moving a donor between bands produces the gap; the bands may contain different kinds of people. The $24.6M-per-point figures size the observed gap, not a treatment effect.
