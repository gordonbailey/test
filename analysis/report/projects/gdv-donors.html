The problem

"Acquire better donors" means nothing until you say what better is. Bigger gifts, donors who stay, donors who bring friends, donors worth more over a lifetime — each one points at a different channel and a different ad.

A better donor is one who gives again. A second gift takes someone from $94.63 to $179.00 a year, and it can be measured in 90 days rather than waiting a year to find out.

This does trade against gift size. Average gift falls as people give more often, from $94.63 to $77.27, so chasing return visits and chasing bigger gifts are different goals.

What was done

Take everyone who donated, and measure how many gave again within 90 days and how long they took. Six months of donation data.

What came out of it

The window is three weeks

23
Days between one gift and the next, at the midpoint. Of everyone who gives again within 90 days, half do it in just over three weeks.
Any program to win a second gift has a one-month window to work in.
  • 12.3% give again within 90 days — 2.49 million of 20.2 million. That is the number to beat.
  • It fits the annual picture: about 20% give twice over a full year, so 12.3% at 90 days is about right.
Giving Wrapped keeps donors, it does not create them. A December recap arrives ten months after a February decision. Build it for people who already came back. It will not get anyone to give a second time — that has to happen in the three-week window.

What a point of lift is worth

Each extra second gift is worth about $89.50. Across roughly 40 million donations a year, moving that 12.3% up by one point is worth around $36M a year.

Treat that as a rough size, not a forecast. It counts donations rather than people, so frequent donors get counted twice, and it assumes the extra gift is worth the average. Good enough to prioritize with. Not good enough for a business case.

What it does not cover

It measures any gift to the next, not first to second

Someone who gave six times gets counted several times over, so the real first-to-second rate is a little below 12.3%.

The 90 days is a tool limit, not a donor behavior

Amplitude will not look further than 90 days. Donors who come back after four months are invisible here, and the annual numbers say they exist — about a third of all repeat giving happens outside this window.

The channel table only sees 59% of donors

41% arrive with no source recorded, and the field that should hold it is full of junk.

Big one-off donors are excluded by design

Someone who gives $5,000 once does not count as a "better donor" here. If the growth answer involves major gifts, this framing misses it entirely.

No cost data

A channel with a 20% return rate that costs four times as much is worse than one at 12%. This data cannot make that comparison.

One method was tried and rejected

A retention query returned 2.6%, which cannot be squared with 20% of donors giving twice a year. The method used here gives 12.3%, which can.

None of this proves cause

These are differences between groups of people, not the measured effect of doing anything.
