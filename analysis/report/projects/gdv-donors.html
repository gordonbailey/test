The problem

"Acquire better donors" needs a definition of better before it can be acted on. Larger gifts, higher retention, more referrals and greater lifetime value point at different channels and different creative.

A better donor is one who gives again. A second gift takes a donor from $94.63 to $179.00 of annual value, and frequency rather than gift size is what separates the bands in the GDV work. It can also be measured in 90 days instead of a year.

This trades off against a gift-size definition. Average gift falls as frequency rises, from $94.63 at one gift to $77.27 at five, so optimizing for return visits and optimizing for gift size are different objectives.

What was done

A two-step funnel on eCommerce - Purchase → eCommerce - Purchase with a 90-day conversion window, over the last six months of consumer donation events, measuring what share of donors give again and how long they take.

What came out of it

The window is three weeks

23
Median days from one gift to the next. Mean 29 days. Of donors who give again inside 90 days, half do it within a little over three weeks.
Any program to win a second gift has a one-month window to work in.
  • 12.3% of donors give again within 90 days, 2.49M of 20.2M. That is the baseline to beat.
  • It squares with the annual picture: about 20% of donors reach 2+ gifts over 365 days, so 12.3% at 90 days is the right order of magnitude.
Giving Wrapped is a retention play, not a conversion lever. An annual recap lands in December for a decision made in February. It is worth building for donors who already came back, and it will not move the 1→2 rate. That job belongs to the three-week window.

What a point of lift is worth

Each additional second gift is worth about $89.50, the average gift in the 2-gift band. Across roughly 40 million donor-anchors a year, one percentage point on the 90-day repeat rate is worth ~$36M annually.

Treat that as an order of magnitude. It counts donor-anchors rather than unique donors, so a frequent donor is counted more than once, and it assumes an incremental gift is worth the band average. Right size for prioritizing, wrong size for a business case.

What it does not cover

The funnel measures gift → next gift, not first → second

Step one is any gift, so a donor with six gifts contributes several anchors and the 12.3% sits slightly above the true first-to-second rate. Isolating genuine first gifts needs an nth-time filter this analysis did not build.

90 days is a tool ceiling, not a behavioral one

Amplitude funnels cap the conversion window at 90 days. Donors who return at 4–12 months are invisible here, and the annual data says they exist: 20% reach 2+ gifts over 365 days against 12.3% at 90. About a third of eventual repeat giving happens outside this window.

Channel comparisons rest on the tagged 59%

With 41% of donors carrying no medium, and the field polluted by Meta ad-set IDs and case duplicates, the channel table describes instrumented traffic rather than all traffic.

The major-gift donor is excluded by construction

Someone who gives $5,000 once is not a better donor under this definition and is not in the super donor cohort. If the growth answer includes major giving, this framing does not reach it.

No acquisition cost

A channel converting at 20% at four times the CPA is worse than one converting at 12%. The ROI comparison an acquisition decision needs is not possible from this data.

Rolling retention was not used

A retention query returned a 2.6% first-period figure that cannot be reconciled with roughly 20% of annual donors reaching 2+ gifts. The funnel construct is the one reported here, and its 12.3% does reconcile.

Nothing here is causal

These are observed differences between populations, not measured effects of an intervention.

Pick this up next

1
Build the three-week intervention with a holdout
The window and the baseline are both known, which is everything a clean test needs. The holdout is what turns $36M per point into a defensible number.
2
Put acquisition cost next to repeat rate
Without CPA the channel table ranks channels on half the equation. This is the biggest gap between this analysis and an acquisition decision.
3
Extend the window past 90 days
A third of eventual repeat giving happens outside what the funnel sees. A warehouse cohort query would close that.
4
Decide whether frequency or gift size is the objective
Average gift falls from $94.63 to $77.27 as frequency rises. Pick one deliberately rather than by whichever KPI gets reported.
