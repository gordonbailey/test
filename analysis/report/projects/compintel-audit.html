Competitive capability audit and gap analysis

Nine vendors, six capabilities, every cell graded on its evidence, plus the gaps nobody occupies. Proprietary & confidential; GoFundMe access required.

The problem

A competitive matrix with blank cells is unreadable: a reader cannot tell "this vendor does not do it" from "nobody checked". And a matrix with a tick in every cell is worse, because it flattens a shipped, generally-available feature and a line in a press release into the same mark.

What was done

Every cell gets one of three grades, and the grade is on the page rather than in a footnote.

GradeWhat it means
ShippedGenerally available and evidenced in public materials, usually with a launch date. Safe to position against.
ClaimedThe vendor says it, and it could not be verified from outside. Blackbaud's up-to-10× capacity claim and Bonterra's 90,000-campaign benchmark depth both sit here.
Not foundAbsent from public materials as of mid-July 2026. Dated evidence, not proof of absence: it may be shipped and unmarketed, in private beta, or on a roadmap.

The four gaps nobody occupies

The useful output of a claim audit is not the filled cells, it is the empty columns. Each of these is unclaimed by all nine vendors.

1Verifiable lift. All nine publish trust and explainability language. None offers lift an organization can check in its own numbers, and none publishes a record of misses.
2Real-time, per-org benchmarking. Peer comparison exists as consultant cohorts, annual studies, quarterly reports and one unverifiable in-product claim. Nobody evidences transaction-level comparison inside the workflow, or an answer for an organization sitting below its peers.
3The autonomy dial. Vendors have picked fixed postures, from suggest-only to fully autonomous. Nobody sells the control itself: customer-set autonomy by segment, channel and gift size.
4Proprietary intelligence. Predictive scoring is being resold through Dataro and DonorSearch, so "we have predictive models" is no longer a claim. What the predictions are trained on is unclaimed ground.
Gap 2 is the one with a working prototype attached: it describes what the benchmarking engine already does. Gap 1 is what the interview research found customers asking for by name.

What it does not cover

No claim was checked against a customer

Every grade is read off public materials. "Shipped" means evidenced and dated, not observed working in a real deployment. The two claimed figures are exactly the kind that shrink on contact with one.

Six capabilities is a choice

They are the ones that come up in deals. Adjacent ground — data hygiene, integrations breadth, service model, pricing flexibility — is out of scope, and a vendor weak on all six could still win on those.

It dates fast

Eight tracked launches in twelve months. Any cell could change without notice, and this one has no re-run date.

Gaps are opportunities, not validated demand

Nobody occupying a space is not evidence that customers want it filled. Gap 2 has customer evidence behind it from the interviews; the other three have none yet.

2

A single reference call with a Blackbaud agent customer would settle the 10× capacity claim, which is the loudest number in the category.
3
Test gap 1 with customers before building for it
Verifiable lift is unclaimed by the whole market and asked for by name in the interviews. That combination is worth a concept test.
