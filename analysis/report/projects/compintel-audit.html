The problem

A competitor table with blank cells is useless, because you cannot tell "they do not do this" from "nobody checked". A table with a tick in every cell is worse: it makes a working feature and a press release look identical.

What was done

Every cell gets one of three grades, shown on the page rather than hidden in a footnote.

GradeWhat it means
ShippedLive, with evidence and usually a launch date. Safe to sell against.
ClaimedThey say so, and there is no way to check from outside. Blackbaud's "10× more productive" and Bonterra's benchmark depth both sit here.
Not foundNowhere to be found in July 2026. That is a dated observation, not proof it does not exist.

The four gaps nobody has taken

The filled cells are not the point. The empty ones are. All nine vendors leave these four open.

1Proof it worked. All nine promise trust. Not one lets a customer check the result in their own numbers, and not one publishes when it got things wrong.
2Live comparison against real peers. It exists as paid consultancy, annual reports, quarterly reports, and one claim nobody can verify. Nobody does it inside the product, and nobody has an answer for an organization sitting below its peers.
3A dial for how much the AI can do. Every vendor has picked one fixed setting, from suggest-only to fully autonomous. Nobody lets the customer choose, donor by donor.
4Predictions built on data nobody else has. Prediction itself is for sale from two suppliers, so "we have models" means nothing. What those models learn from is still open ground.
Gap 2 already has a working prototype: the benchmarking engine. Gap 1 is what customers asked for by name in the interviews.

What it does not cover

No claim was checked with a customer

Every grade comes off public materials. "Shipped" means there is evidence and a date, not that anyone watched it work.

Six capabilities is a choice

These are the ones that come up in deals. Support, integrations and pricing are out of scope, and a vendor that scores badly on all six could still beat us on those.

It goes stale fast

Eight launches in twelve months. Any cell could change without warning, and nobody has set a date to redo this.

An empty gap is not proven demand

Nobody being there does not mean customers want it. Gap 2 has interview evidence behind it. The other three have none.

Nobody in the market offers proof it worked, and customers asked for it by name. That combination is worth testing properly.
