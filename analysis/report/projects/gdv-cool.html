The problem

Giving on GoFundMe is episodic. Donors arrive for a crisis and disappear, and nothing attaches identity, status or habit to being generous. Marketplace is a first attempt, but a donation is not inherently cool. The experience around giving is where coolness can live.

What was done

Second-gift rate within 90 days, split by the campaign_medium on the first gift, across the last six months of donation events. Repeat giving is the proxy for habit.

The split is interpersonal versus broadcast. A donor who arrived through a link a person handed them — chat, copy-link, email, referral — returns at 19–28%. A donor who arrived through organic social returns at 10.6%, below the 12.3% average. Broadcast social is the worst-retaining channel measured.
Fund broadcast and interpersonal against different metrics. Celebrity and creator reach buys first-gift volume. Person-to-person sharing builds repeat donors, at 2.2× baseline. Giving circles are the productised version of the channel that already works.

The productised answer: in-person community events

The most interpersonal thing available is putting people in a room together. Three truths sit underneath it, and none is about giving.

Truth 1

People want to spend time together, in person

Giving today is a solitary act performed on a phone, which is the least social possible expression of a social impulse.

Truth 2

People want to have fun

A run, a dinner, a show, a match, a pub quiz. The event carries the enjoyment and the giving rides along with it. Nobody attends a fundraiser for fun; plenty of people attend a race that happens to raise money.

Truth 3

People want meaning and purpose

GoFundMe owns this outright. Eventbrite, Strava and Meetup all have to manufacture meaning; here it is the default, and it is currently unused.

Frame them as community events, not fundraisers, and capture the donation in the room. "Fundraiser" makes the donation the price of entry. The donation should be the thing that happens because you were there.

The enabling capability is already built

Tap to Donate came out of the hackathon as the top-ranked build, and it solves in-person capture:

DemandMost-voted request in the book, 204+ customers, open since December 2014.
Named wedgeThe Salvation Army's ~$100M offline year-end campaign, where QR codes already failed on friction.
EffortTwo sprints, one person, per the lead engineer. No hardware: Apple and Google tap-to-pay inside the Live Events app.
CaveatThat estimate excludes PCI and Tap-to-Pay certification, which is the real gate.

The two projects need each other: events give the capability a reason to exist beyond one enterprise account, and the capability makes the events measurable. Full scope on the hackathon page.

How to test it. Run one instrumented pilot event: capture donations in the room through Tap to Donate, tag the attendees, and measure their 90-day second-gift rate against the 12.3% baseline and the 27% copy-link cohort. Copy-link-grade retention supports the interpersonal thesis. Baseline retention says the effect is about relationships rather than proximity, and the strategy changes.

What the data supports

Supported

Donors who arrive through an interpersonal channel are about twice as likely to give again within 90 days as the platform average, and about 2.6× as likely as donors arriving through organic social. Repeat giving concentrates in person-to-person sharing.

Not supported

That adding share buttons or launching giving circles produces a 2× lift. The channel is probably a marker of relationship strength rather than a cause of it: someone who gets a link from a friend is already closer to the cause than a stranger scrolling a feed.

What it does not cover

Selection rather than causation

The best-performing channels are the ones a friend uses to reach you, and those donors would plausibly have retained better through any mechanism. Nothing here separates "interpersonal channels build habits" from "people with existing relationships use interpersonal channels." The way through is an experiment that randomises the prompt to share, not the channel.

The best cells are the smallest

chat is n=699 and organic_social is n=1,053 against a 20.2M baseline. Only social (128,844), partner (76,659), email (69,867), referral (34,995) and the two large buckets carry planning-grade volume. The 28.3% headline is direction, not a forecast.

41% of donors have no medium

8.3M (none) plus 19,468 undefined out of 20.2M, so the comparison is drawn from the tagged 59%. Tagged traffic skews toward campaigns someone bothered to instrument. It is the consumer-side view of what the UTM project fixes and what the interview research found on the nonprofit side, where 96% of Salvation Army transactions carry no referral data.

The medium field is dirty enough to affect the read

Meta ad-set identifiers are leaking into campaign_medium as values like [Worldwide]|120239607932490314. Email and email are separate values. customer,social is a concatenation rather than one medium. Some of what reads as broadcast may be mislabelled, and the reverse.

Repeat gift is a narrow proxy for cool

Second-gift rate measures habit. It says nothing about whether giving became identity-forming or socially visible, which is what the strategy reaches for. A donor could return twice a year and feel nothing about being a generous person.

No brand or attitudinal measurement

Everything here is behavioural. Whether giving is perceived as cool, by whom, and against what, has not been asked. No survey, no brand tracker, no audience segmentation.

Pick this up next

1
Instrument sharing before building anything
Copy-link is the best-performing real-volume channel and it is barely tagged: 13,985 donors when the true number is far higher.
2
Run giving circles as an experiment, not a launch
Randomise the invitation to give alongside someone, hold out a control, and read second-gift rate at 90 days. That turns a 2× correlation into a treatment effect, or kills it cheaply.
3
Pilot one community event with Tap to Donate
In-person capture plus tagged attendees is the only way to test whether proximity or relationship is doing the work.
4
Add an attitudinal measure
If the goal is that giving feels cool, behaviour alone will not confirm it. One question in an existing survey — would you tell a friend you did this — gets further than another funnel query.
