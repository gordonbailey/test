Two gaps

Donation sizes are up while donor counts are flat to down. That is growth getting narrower, and both gaps below are a version of it.

Gap 01

Giving isn't cool

Giving here is episodic. Donors show up for a crisis and disappear. Nothing attaches identity, status or habit to being generous. Marketplace is a first attempt, but "does this make giving cool?" belongs as a lens on every product decision, not one product's job.

Gap 02

Nobody owns super donors

Donors who give 2+ times a year produce 39% of GDV from 20% of the donor base and get the same experience as a first-time donor. No owner, no lifecycle engagement, no recognition.

Limits

None of this is causal

Every figure describes what donor populations already do. None of it shows that an intervention moves a donor between bands, and the bands may contain different kinds of people. The dollar figures size an observed gap, not a measured treatment effect. Every page leads its next steps with the experiment that would close that distance.

The strongest rates come from the smallest samples

The 28.3% chat referral rate is n=699 against a 20.2M baseline. Treat it as direction, not a forecast. Only a handful of channels carry planning-grade volume.

41% of donors cannot be attributed

8.3M donors carry no referral medium, and the field is polluted by Meta ad-set IDs and case duplicates. Every channel comparison here describes instrumented traffic, not all traffic. It is the consumer-side face of what the UTM project fixes.

Three GDV totals are in circulation

$1.55B in the working spreadsheet, $1.74B in the deck, $1.775B from Amplitude. The shares hold across all three, so the 20/39 split is safe, but the absolute dollars need reconciling to one source. Worked through on the GDV page.
