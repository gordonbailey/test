Two gaps

People are giving more each time, but there are not more of them. Growth is coming from a shrinking group. Both gaps below are a version of that problem.

A note on the numbers: GDV means gross donation volume — the total amount donated through the platform.

Gap 01

Giving isn't cool

People give once, during a crisis, and disappear. Nothing about the experience makes generosity feel like part of who they are. Marketplace is a first attempt, but "does this make giving cool?" should be a question we ask of every product decision, not one team's job.

Gap 02

Nobody owns super donors

Donors who give twice or more produce 39% of the money from 20% of the people, and get exactly the same experience as someone giving for the first time. Nobody owns them, nothing recognizes them.

Limits

None of this is causal

Every figure describes what donors already do. None of it proves that doing something would move anyone from one group to another — the groups may simply contain different kinds of people. The dollar figures size a gap we can see, not an effect anyone has measured. Every page leads its next steps with the experiment that would settle it.

The strongest rates come from the smallest samples

The 28.3% chat referral rate comes from 699 people against a base of 20.2 million. Treat it as a direction, not a forecast.

41% of donors cannot be attributed

8.3 million donors arrive with no source recorded, and the field that should hold it is full of junk. Every channel comparison here describes only the traffic we can see. It is the same problem the UTM project fixes.

Three GDV totals are in circulation

$1.55B in the spreadsheet, $1.74B in the deck, $1.775B from Amplitude. The percentages hold across all three, so the 20/39 split is safe. The dollar totals need reconciling to one source.
