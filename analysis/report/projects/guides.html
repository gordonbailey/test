Why these exist

Everything else in this portfolio is analysis: a question asked and answered. These two are reference material, written to be reusable by someone who was not here. The test is whether a new product manager, in their first week, can get a useful answer out of one without asking anybody and without being confidently misled.

What makes a handover guide work

State your confidence on every claim

In the repo guide, ownership is labelled documented, inferred or contested, and the 271 repos it could not classify are shown as unclassified rather than forced into a cluster. A guide that hides its uncertainty gets trusted during an incident and sends someone to the wrong place.

Teach the question, not just the answer

The non-functional guide gives every requirement the questions to ask engineering and the spec language to write down. The repo guide's wayfinder walks the same path a patient senior engineer would. Neither aims to make the reader an expert, only to make the next conversation a competent one.

Hosting is most of the value

Both are single self-contained HTML files, which is why they work offline and why they can disappear at handover. Both now run inside their own page here, and the source files sit in the repo alongside this site. What is still missing is an internal URL an engineer can open mid-incident — the same risk visible in the UTM tickets, where a referenced Confluence page sits in someone's personal space.
