The problem

The 39 interviews sat in 39 separate documents. Research in that shape gets quoted selectively and never adds up to a decision. This pulls everything about AI, reporting and analytics into one place and looks for what repeats.

Customers do not want more AI. They want AI they can control, understand and check. And reporting, which nobody had called a strategic problem, turned out to be the biggest source of pain in the whole dataset.

What came out of it

Four findings. Three are about trust. The fourth is demand that ChatGPT is already meeting.

1 · Intelligent Ask: they want it, they do not trust it

Intelligent Ask suggests a donation amount to each donor. Nobody rejected it, and nobody will rely on it until they can control it and see how it works. Five things came up again and again.

2 · AI writing: small charities love it, it keeps breaking

Where demand is strong

  • AI-drafted project stories for teachers with no fundraising experience — Oakland Public Ed Fund.
  • Rapid AI-assisted launch for one-month fundraising windows — MSSM Foundation.
  • "Heartfelt or professional" appeal drafts with room for a personal story — New Breeds.
  • Image and video generation aligned to the org's style guide — BWI.
  • Founders already use ChatGPT and Copilot for appeals, blogs, even CSV cleanup for imports.

Where trust is breaking

  • AI video generation has never once worked for Feed My Starving Children, despite repeated troubleshooting.
  • Vine Maple Place now refuses every beta after one bad experience, which delays features that would help them.
  • Experienced fundraisers do not trust anything automatic they cannot look inside.
  • One broken AI feature damages adoption of every AI feature.

Nobody can tell where their donations came from

96%
72,000 of 75,000 donations at The Salvation Army arrive with no source attached, so almost no gift can be traced back to what prompted it.
"It's difficult to determine the ROI of Facebook ads or YouTube videos."
  • The tracking tags cannot be exported. Feed My Starving Children cannot get at the tags in its own links, and only the last click is recorded, so the rest of the donor's journey is invisible.
  • Sources get lost part-way through. They drop when someone sets up a personal fundraising page, and when a donor crosses between our site and the charity's own.
  • Nobody knows what a donor costs to win. Agencies call working it out "a long process" of exports matched by hand against ad spend.
This is why the UTM and Instrumentation projects exist. What customers are describing here is exactly what those two fix.

They asked for benchmarks, so benchmarks got built. "A conversion rate with no context" is exactly what the benchmarking engine answers.

What it does not cover

The counts are not percentages

"Six organizations asked for this" means six of 33 raised it without being prompted. That is strong evidence it matters, and no evidence at all about what share of customers agree. Nothing here was sized with a survey.

Two groups are too small to stand alone

Agency partners and solo operators are two interviews each. Anything that shows up only there is an anecdote until it is checked elsewhere.

One headline claim is my interpretation, not something a customer said

"Low traffic to the reports page means avoidance, not low need" reconciles the usage data with what people described. Everyone did say they export to Excel instead. It is still a hypothesis and needs a direct test.

Nobody was asked what they would pay

This shows what people want and how much effort they waste. It says nothing about willingness to pay, or who would switch platforms.

The people who agreed to talk are not a random sample

Anyone who gives up an hour for a research call is engaged. The customers quietly exporting everything and shopping around are the ones missing from this.

Pick this up next

1
Let people control Intelligent Ask
A floor and a ceiling first — it is the single most-requested thing in the dataset. Then a choice of goal, and plain-English documentation of where the numbers come from.
2
Prove the AI works, inside the product
Built-in testing for every AI feature, then fix or retire the unreliable ones. One broken feature is poisoning trust in all of them.
3
Fix reporting
Comparison against last year, combined reports, the missing monthly-giving filters, saved views, folders and scheduled delivery. The biggest source of pain and the least glamorous work on this list.
4
Fix tracking, and serve the small charities
Keep the source attached through personal fundraising pages and site handoffs. And bring story drafting and appeal writing into the product for the people already doing it in ChatGPT.
Bigger customers will adopt AI once they can control it and check it. Smaller ones already want it, and ChatGPT is meeting that need today.
