The problem

The 39 interviews existed as separate write-ups, one document at a time, which is the shape of research that gets quoted selectively and never adds up to a decision. So this pulls everything in them touching AI, intelligence, reporting and analytics into one place and looks for what repeats.

The unmet need is not more AI. Customers will adopt AI once it is controllable, explainable and measurable. And the reporting layer, which nobody had flagged as a strategic problem, is the single largest pain cluster in the dataset.

What came out of it

Four findings. The first three are about trust; the fourth is demand already being served elsewhere.

2 · AI content — loved by small orgs, unreliable in practice

Where demand is strong

  • AI-drafted project stories for teachers with no fundraising experience — Oakland Public Ed Fund.
  • Rapid AI-assisted launch for one-month fundraising windows — MSSM Foundation.
  • "Heartfelt or professional" appeal drafts with room for a personal story — New Breeds.
  • Image and video generation aligned to the org's style guide — BWI.
  • Founders already use ChatGPT and Copilot for appeals, blogs, even CSV cleanup for imports.

Where trust is breaking

  • AI video generation has never worked for FMSC despite troubleshooting — feeding an "MVP rollout" perception.
  • Vine Maple Place now refuses all betas after "beta trauma", delaying adoption of features that would help them.
  • Veteran fundraisers distrust automated features they cannot inspect — CalMatters.
  • One broken AI feature damages adoption of every AI feature.

Attribution is broken at scale

96%
72,000 of 75,000 transactions at The Salvation Army are missing referral data, so almost no gift can be tied to a channel or an ROI.
"It's difficult to determine the ROI of Facebook ads or YouTube videos."
  • UTMs cannot be exported. FMSC cannot capture or export the UTM parameters in its own tracking links; only last-touch attribution is available, so full donor journeys are invisible.
  • Source codes drop mid-journey. Lost during P2P fundraiser setup (Americares) and when users cross between the platform and the org's own website (Akshaya Patra), forcing manual CRM workaround logic.
  • Acquisition cost is guesswork. Agencies describe calculating true donor-acquisition cost as "a long process" of manual exports and supporter-ID comparisons against ad spend (Community Boost).
This is the case for the instrumentation and UTM work. The attribution failures here are the customer-side symptom of what those two projects address — Instrumentation and UTM.

Benchmarks were asked for by name, and then built. "A conversion rate with no context" is precisely the problem the fundraising benchmarking engine solves: it puts every organization against peers of the same cause and size, and turns the gap into a ranked list of actions.

What it does not cover

The counts are not prevalence

"Requested by 6+ orgs" means six organisations out of 33 raised it unprompted. That is strong signal it matters and no evidence about what share of the book agrees. Nothing here has been sized with a survey.

Two segments are too thin to stand alone

Agency partners and Solo Operators are two interviews each, so a finding appearing only there is an anecdote until checked elsewhere. Self-Serve (12), Mid-Market (10) and Enterprise (8) carry enough weight to compare; Essentials (7) is borderline.

One headline claim is an inference, not something a customer said

"Low My Reports traffic means avoidance, not lack of need" is an interpretation that reconciles the usage data with the interview evidence. Every persona does describe exporting to Excel instead, but it remains a hypothesis about why a number is low and needs a direct test.

Nobody was asked what they would pay

The research establishes want and quantifies wasted effort. It says nothing about willingness to pay, or which of these customers would switch platforms.

Personas are constructs, not a segmentation

The nine were coded from workflow after the fact. They help in reasoning about who is affected, and they are not a validated segmentation model — no organisation is assigned to one in any system.

Participation was not random

Interviewees were recruited, not sampled. Organisations willing to spend an hour on a research call skew engaged, which likely understates the pain of disengaged accounts — the ones already exporting everything and quietly evaluating alternatives.

Pick this up next

1
Make Intelligent Ask controllable
Min/max guardrails, in-form segmentation, a choice of optimisation goal, org-defined ask logic, gift-string matching, and plain-language documentation of where the numbers come from. Guardrails are the most-requested item in the dataset.
2
Prove AI value natively
Built-in A/B testing and lift reporting for every AI feature, then fix or retire the unreliable ones. AI video has never worked for FMSC, and one broken feature is poisoning trust in all of them.
3
Close the reporting gap
Year-over-year and cross-campaign comparison, combined reports, first-installment and active-plan filters, AND/OR logic, saved views, folders, scheduled delivery, in-product visualisation and benchmarks. The largest pain cluster and the least glamorous.
4
Fix attribution, and serve grassroots
Persist source codes and UTMs through P2P and site transitions. Bring story drafting, appeal writing and import cleanup in-product for the Essentials users already doing it in ChatGPT.
Enterprise and mid-market will adopt AI once they can control and verify it. Grassroots demand already exists, and ChatGPT is serving it today.
