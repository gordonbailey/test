The problem

A hackathon produces enthusiasm, not a roadmap. Sixteen projects came out of this one, each with an owner who believed in it, and no shared basis for comparing a payments feature against a reliability fix against an AI reporting tool. Roadmap capacity is finite and the 2026 strategy has eleven priorities, so the question is which projects earn a slot, and what has to be true first.

The two ways to get this wrong are funding everything that demoed well, and parking everything without a champion in the room. Both produce a roadmap that reflects who presented best.

What was done

1
Score all 16 on the same three things
GDV impact, churn prevention, feasibility
2
Merge the duplicates before ranking
Ivy + Comparative Insights → Reporting Hub; Campaign AI Chat + GoDraftMe Pro → Campaign Copilot. Comparative Insights alone reads as a nice-to-have; folded in as the Hub's flagship it is the reason the Hub is worth building
3
Get an effort estimate from whoever wrote the code
Sprint counts and caveats in the owner's own words
4
Check each candidate against the 2026 priorities
A project with no priority to sit under has no sponsor
5
Tier into build, validate and park
Every parked project carries the condition that would revive it

Themes across all sixteen

Mergers

Overlapping ideas kept resolving into one better one. Ivy + Comparative → Reporting Hub is the clearest case.

Insights

Most Pro ideas circled the same job: help customers make better decisions from their own data.

Defense

The strongest cases were protective. Reporting Hub and Live Events both defend GDV rather than add it.

Against the customer-facing jobs, the sixteen cluster into raise more money, work more efficiently and reach more supporters, with recurring sub-themes of revenue per donor, turning supporters into advocates, and enterprise-grade operational confidence.

What it does not cover

The GDV figures are modeled ranges, not forecasts

Bear, base and bull scenarios built to compare projects on one scale. None has been through finance, and the bull cases assume adoption nobody has measured.

Two deck figures were self-inconsistent and are now fixed

Smart Retry's slide headlined $6M a year against a table showing base $1.5M; the table was right. Tap to Donate's upside read $31M on its slide and $30M in the table. This page carries the corrected numbers.

Sprint counts exclude what gates a launch

Tap to Donate's two sprints exclude PCI and Tap-to-Pay certification. Live Events' two sprints are real, but the team's December project is the binding constraint.

Three projects have no usable estimate

ScriptGuard has none by the owner's own admission, TuneAI none after two doc handoffs, and KMP needs a spike first. Ranking them beside costed projects gives them false precision.

Reporting Hub overlaps work already in flight

Insights Driven Homepage and the Recurring Insights Dashboard cover adjacent ground. Someone has to decide what the Hub subsumes.

No customer was asked to price any of this

Demand evidence is interview-derived and ACV-adjacent: $195M of Strategic mentions for Thank You Gifts, 204+ votes for Tap to Donate. That establishes want, not willingness to pay or to operate.
