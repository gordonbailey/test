The problem

A hackathon produces enthusiasm, not a roadmap. Sixteen projects came out of this one, each with an owner who believed in it, and no shared basis for comparing a payments feature against a reliability fix against an AI reporting tool. Roadmap capacity is finite and the 2026 strategy has eleven priorities — so the question was not "which of these is good?" but which of these earn a slot, and what has to be true first.

Two failure modes to avoid. Funding everything that demoed well, and parking everything that did not have a champion in the room. Both produce a roadmap that reflects who presented best rather than what moves GDV.

What was done

1
Score every project on the same three things
GDV impact, churn prevention, feasibility — applied to all 16, not just the memorable ones
2
Merge the duplicates before ranking
Ivy + Comparative Insights → Reporting Hub; Campaign AI Chat + GoDraftMe Pro → Campaign Copilot
3
Get an effort estimate from the person who built it
Owner-supplied sprint counts and the caveats behind them, in the owner's own words
4
Check each candidate against the 2026 priorities
A project with no priority to sit under is a project without a sponsor
5
Tier into build / validate / park, each with a named next step
Parked is not killed — every parked project carries the condition that would revive it
Why the merges came first. Ranking sixteen items when four of them are two ideas each inflates whatever gets ranked twice, and splits the vote for whichever half is weaker. Comparative Insights on its own looks like a nice-to-have; folded into the Reporting Hub as its flagship feature it is the reason the hub is worth building.

Themes across all sixteen

Mergers

Overlapping ideas kept resolving into one better one. Ivy + Comparative → Reporting Hub is the clearest case.

Insights

Most Pro ideas circled the same job: help customers make better decisions from their own data.

Defense

The strongest cases were protective. Reporting Hub and Live Events both defend GDV rather than add it.

Mapped against the customer-facing jobs, the sixteen cluster into raise more money, work more efficiently and reach more supporters — with the recurring sub-themes of maximizing revenue per donor, turning supporters into advocates, and enterprise-grade operational confidence.

What it does not cover

Stated so the next reader does not mistake a modelled range for a commitment.

The GDV figures are modelled ranges, not forecasts

Bear / base / bull scenarios built to compare projects against each other on one scale. None has been through finance, and the bull cases in particular assume adoption nobody has measured yet.

Two internal inconsistencies were found in review — both since corrected in the deck

Smart Retry's slide headlined $6M/yr while the combined-impact table gave bear $0.7M, base $1.5M, bull $2.8M and labelled the row "new GDV from campaign lift" — wording that appeared to belong to a different project. Tap to Donate's upside read $31M on its own slide and $30M in the table. Both have been fixed in the linked deck. The Smart Retry figures on this page still show the pre-correction table values and need re-syncing; treat the deck as the source of truth until they are updated here.

Sprint counts exclude the things that actually gate a launch

Tap to Donate's two sprints explicitly exclude PCI and Tap-to-Pay certification. Live Events' two sprints are real but the team's December project is the binding constraint. Neither is an engineering estimate problem.

Three projects have no usable estimate at all

ScriptGuard has none by the owner's own admission. TuneAI has none after two doc handoffs. KMP needs a spike before an estimate is even possible. Ranking them alongside costed projects gives them a false precision.

Reporting Hub overlaps work already in flight

Insights Driven Homepage and the Recurring Insights Dashboard both cover adjacent ground. The overlap is flagged in the deck but not resolved — someone has to decide what the Hub subsumes.

No customer was asked to price any of this

Demand evidence is interview-derived and ACV-adjacent ($195M of Strategic mentions for Thank You Gifts, 204+ votes for Tap to Donate). That establishes want, not willingness to pay or to operate.
