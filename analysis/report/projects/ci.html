The question

How do we tell a customer something true about their own fundraising that they could not work out on their own?

A nonprofit can see its total raised, its average gift, its conversion rate — and has no way to tell whether any of it is good. City of Hope asked exactly that in an interview: is this a good conversion rate? Without an answer, an account manager has an opinion instead of a recommendation.

A platform average is no help, because a $2M food bank and a $200M hospital foundation are not the same business. The comparison has to hold cause and size steady, and it has to end in something the organization can do.

The answer, built and running. 3,161 accounts are grouped with organizations genuinely like them — same cause, same size — and scored inside that group. Each one gets a percentile, a gap in dollars, and up to three things to try, ranked by expected lift. Open the benchmarking engine.

They ran separately and found the same gap

Customers asked for benchmarks by name, and nobody sells them

Peer benchmarking exists in the market in four shapes: consultant cohorts, annual studies, quarterly reports, and one in-product claim nobody can verify. No vendor shows real-time comparison inside the workflow, and none has an answer for an organization sitting below its peers.

"Prove it worked" is the customer's ask and the market's blind spot

Vine Maple Place turned Intelligent Ask on and could not tell whether it helped. Cedars-Sinai had its worst Giving Tuesday after two changes at once and could not tell which one did it. All nine competitors publish trust language; not one offers lift an organization can check in its own numbers.

Attribution is where customer pain becomes an engineering problem

96% of Salvation Army transactions carry no referral data. Feed My Starving Children cannot export the tracking tags in its own links. That is what Instrumentation and UTM fix, which is why those two sit outside this group but belong to the same argument.

Read the three together with care. The interviews are qualitative and recruited, the competitive scan reads vendor marketing rather than behavior, and the benchmarking engine runs on one CRM export. Three sources with three different weaknesses agreeing is encouraging, not proof.
